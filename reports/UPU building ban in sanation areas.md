# A sanation label, not new law, would freeze building

*Research report, 27 September 2026; figures updated 28 September 2026 (see the end of Q1). The question comes from a resident of east Split (Dračevac, Mostine, Bilice). The permit evidence also covers the neighbouring eastern suburbs.*

The resident has the facts essentially right, and the puzzle has a precise answer: **a ban would come from a new label on the same land, not from a new law**. In east Split, **93% of the area where the April 2025 draft makes building wait for a UPU** (urbanistički plan uređenja, a detailed neighbourhood plan) **already waits under map 4.c of the GUP in force**, and 99% of the draft's "urbana sanacija" land lies inside the UPU obligation in force today. National law has barred permits for new buildings until a UPU is adopted **since 1 January 2014**, and the new Zakon o prostornom uređenju (NN 155/25, in force since 1 January 2026) keeps that bar. The bar, however, applies only to land a plan designates as neuređeno (unserviced), urbana preobrazba (transformation) or urbana sanacija (sanation), and **the GUP in force (SGGS 55/14) never gave residential east Split those labels**. Read closely, the GUP's own text does route new houses there through the missing UPU. The City has not applied that reading: the Ministry's register shows **about 57 approvals for new residential buildings in such areas from 2016 to September 2026, six of them in Dračevac**, and no refusal based on the missing plan. The draft would call the same land "urbana sanacija" or "neuređeno". That turns a plan-made obligation, which the permit office has treated as porous, into a condition of the national permit law that the office cannot read away, and the draft also removes the GUP's own routes for extending existing houses. **That label, and the choice of a UPU over UPU-level detail in the GUP itself, are the City's choices; the law allows either.** Citywide the labels cover 681 ha. What they would hold back is the part of it still free for a new building: 206 ha, of which 132 ha lies on 1,730 parcels with nothing built on them. Building did continue, though less than it may seem: about 4% of the buildings in the east-Split waiting area are new since 2017, and legalisations of houses built before 2011 outnumber new-build permits by more than 15 to 1. The draft is not adopted, so nothing is banned today. The City's call for GUP initiatives, open from 1 October to 16 November 2026, is the moment to ask for a different label.

Four instruments matter here, and only two of them bind today.

## Q1. Are the areas really the same? Yes: 93% of the draft's waiting area already waits

**Answer: yes for east Split, almost exactly. Where the two maps differ, the draft is looser, not stricter.**

The comparison uses the project site's own model of both plans. Under the GUP in force, new building "waits" where three conditions meet: the land lies inside a UPU or DPU obligation on map 4.c, it falls under a low-consolidated (niskokonsolidirano) urban rule 3.x on the urban-rules sheet, and no plan is in force there (čl. 104–105). Under the draft, building waits where map 4.d fills the land as sanacija, preobrazba or neuređeno outside a plan in force (čl. 103(1)). Plans in force are taken at their real boundaries, from each plan's own sheets on ISPU, not from the schematic hatch of map 4.d. A blue UPU outline with no fill is only a "preporuka" (recommendation), and building there goes ahead directly. All three sheets were rasterised on a 2 m grid from the City's PDFs. Their median offset from the official plan outlines is 4–7 m, and shifting either mask by 10 m changes the shares below by at most two percentage points ([map 4.c, in force](https://split.hr/strateski-dokumenti/prostorno-planska-dokumentacija/planovi-na-snazi/gis-podatci?EntryId=3179&Command=Core_Download); [urban-rules sheet 4.b](https://split.hr/strateski-dokumenti/prostorno-planska-dokumentacija/planovi-na-snazi/gis-podatci?EntryId=3197&Command=Core_Download); [draft map 4.d](https://split.hr/Portals/0/Dokumenti/Prostorno-planska/Izmjene%20i%20dopune%20Generalnog%20urbanisti%C4%8Dkog%20plana%20Splita%20za%20ponovnu%20javnu%20raspravu/4_d%20Podrucja%20i%20dijelovi%20primjene%20planskih%20mjera%20zastite.pdf)).

The study window covers 187.3 ha: the Dračevac and Bilice kvarts plus the draft's UPU 17 Mostine, UPU 18 Dračevac 2 and UPU 19 Harakovac. In that window the in-force UPU outline and the draft's UPU outlines are nearly identical. The **Jaccard index is 0.98** (overlap divided by union, where 1.0 means identical): 137.2 ha are shared out of 137.3 ha and 140.1 ha. **93% of the draft's waiting area already waits under the GUP in force.** **99.4% of the draft's urbana sanacija lies inside the in-force UPU obligation**, and 74% of it is urban rule 3.1. In the draft's UPU Dračevac 2, 97% of the land is rule 3.1.

By the site's land-use raster, that land is mostly business-zoned (I/K) land in Mostine and Harakovac, a share not yet checked against the legend. **The draft adds only 7.8 ha of new waiting land**, almost all of it on the edge of the Karepovac city project. ## Q2. What does the GUP in force say? On paper, new houses wait, and have since 2006

**Answer: the better reading of SGGS 55/14 is that a new house on a rule 3.1 plot inside a map 4.c boundary cannot be permitted directly under the GUP.** Until the UPU exists, a legal building may only be reconstructed or change use within its existing dimensions. The mechanism dates from the GUP's adoption in January 2006, not from 2008. However, the text has a genuinely ambiguous phrase that supports the opposite reading, and nothing authoritative has settled it.

The provisions work together. **Čl. 105** sets "obveza izrade urbanističkih i provedbenih dokumenata prostornog uređenja za obuhvate prema kartografskom prikazu … 4.c", the obligation to make plans for the areas on map 4.c. It then allows, "do donošenja provedbenih dokumenata" (until those plans are adopted), permits only for a closed list: parts of the street network, traffic and utility infrastructure, port works, public buildings, small recreation areas, and production or business buildings on plots of at least 3,000 m² after a competition. Houses are not on the list.

**Čl. 104** treats the two kinds of area differently. In consolidated areas it allows building "temeljem ovog Plana" (on the basis of this Plan) without qualification. In low-consolidated areas it allows that route only "za područja za koja nije propisana izrada provedbenog dokumenta prostornog uređenja" (for areas where no plan is prescribed).

**Rule 3.1** (čl. 73) is titled "Sanacija, uređivanje i urbana obnova djelomično izgrađenih prostora mješovite izgradnje" (sanation, servicing and urban renewal of partly built mixed areas). For M1 zones it reads: *"Omogućava se nova izgradnja, zamjena postojećih građevina i rekonstrukcija postojećih građevina te uređenje javnih prostora uz izradu provedbenog dokumenta prostornog uređenja, ukoliko je ovim odredbama utvrđena obveza izrade provedbenog dokumenta, a za ostalo temeljem ovog Plana uz slijedeće uvjete"*. In English: new building, replacement and reconstruction go through the plan where these provisions set an obligation, "and for the rest on the basis of this Plan", under the following conditions.

**Čl. 49** adds a transitional rule for existing buildings: *"Do odnošenja* [sic, for donošenja] *provedbenih dokumenata prostornog uređenja moguća je rekonstrukcija i prenamjena legalnih građevina u postojećim gabaritima"*, meaning reconstruction only within existing dimensions until the plans are adopted ([GUP SGGS 55/14](https://split.hr/strateski-dokumenti/prostorno-planska-dokumentacija/planovi-na-snazi/gis-podatci?EntryId=6130&Command=Core_Download)).

### The case for "forbidden" is stronger on the text

The strongest arguments are structural. Čl. 104 gives consolidated areas the "temeljem ovog Plana" route freely but gives it to low-consolidated areas only where no plan is prescribed, and a 3.1 plot inside a 4.c boundary is low-consolidated and has a prescribed plan. Čl. 105 is phrased as a positive grant, so what it omits waits.

Rule 3.1's own carve-outs would be pointless if 3.1 plots could already be built before the plan. T1 tourism is "moguća realizacija temeljem ovog Plana, prije donošenja propisanog provedbenog dokumenta" (possible under this Plan before the prescribed plan is adopted), and similar clauses cover the OŠ Stobreč sports hall, the Stobreč shooting range and, elsewhere in the plan, hotel P29. Čl. 49 also allows adding storeys to legal houses only "izvan obuhvata" (outside plan boundaries).

The drafting history points the same way. The 2008 amendment created the "za ostalo" wording by replacing the words "(u granicama obuhvata utvrđenim ovim Planom)" ("within the boundaries set by this Plan"). The replacement is therefore geographic: "ostalo" means the rest of the 3.1 land, where no obligation applies ([GUP SGGS 3/08](https://split.hr/strateski-dokumenti/prostorno-planska-dokumentacija/planovi-na-snazi/gis-podatci?EntryId=3183&Command=Core_Download)).

The City's own words support this reading. The April 2025 Obrazloženje (explanatory report) says that in some areas "za koja je dosadašnjim GUP-om bila obvezna izrada … UPU-a … više se ne može propisati ta obveza, tako da gradnja postaje moguća izravno temeljem GUP-a". In English: where the current GUP required a UPU, building *becomes* possible directly under the GUP. The passage is about areas that lose the obligation, but it assumes that an obligation in force blocks direct building ([Obrazloženje, April 2025, p. 2](https://split.hr/Portals/0/Dokumenti/Prostorno-planska/Izmjene%20i%20dopune%20Generalnog%20urbanisti%C4%8Dkog%20plana%20Splita%20za%20ponovnu%20javnu%20raspravu/3_%20Obrazlozenje.pdf)).

The City has also enforced a plan-made obligation where a rule states it explicitly. In 2016 it told an owner in a consolidated rule 2.7 zone that no permit was possible because "GUP-om (članak 70.) predviđena obveza izrade detaljnijeg plana" (the GUP provides for a more detailed plan), as recorded in consultation objection no. 70 ([Izvješće o javnoj raspravi, 26 Mar 2025, pp. 187–188](https://split.hr/DesktopModules/EasyDNNNews/DocumentDownload.ashx?portalid=0&moduleid=2192&articleid=20457&documentid=15067)).

In Harakovac the text is clearer still. The land there falls almost entirely under rules 3.2–3.6, chiefly rule 3.2, "Nova regulacija na pretežito neizgrađenom prostoru" (new layout on mostly unbuilt land). Rule 3.2 requires the plan and has no "za ostalo" clause.

### The case for "allowed" is weaker, but real

The "allowed" reading starts from grammar. Rule 3.1's sentence can be parsed so that only public spaces need the plan, while plots ("ostalo") are built directly under the listed conditions. Those conditions look written for direct permits: they are plot-level, down to an infill clause, "ukoliko se nova (jedna) građevna parcela formira između dvije izgrađene parcele Ppmin=300 m²" (if a single new plot is formed between two built plots, minimum 300 m²).

Two sentences in čl. 49 support it too. The first is unqualified: "U nisko konsolidiranom području omogućava se dogradnja i nadogradnja postojećih, legalnih građevina prema urbanim pravilima" (in low-consolidated areas, extending and raising legal buildings is allowed under the urban rules). It contains no "outside plan boundaries" limit. The second offers relaxed conditions for built parts "kroz izradu propisanog provedbenog dokumenta … ili na drugi način utvrđen ovom Odlukom" (through the prescribed plan, or in another way set by this Decision) ([GUP SGGS 55/14](https://split.hr/strateski-dokumenti/prostorno-planska-dokumentacija/planovi-na-snazi/gis-podatci?EntryId=6130&Command=Core_Download)).

Most tellingly, the City's reply to objection no. 73 described rule 3.1's maximum-footprint parameter as a "mehanizam zaštite prostora do donošenja planova užeg područja", a mechanism protecting the area until the narrower plans are adopted. That only makes sense if plots are being built under it before those plans ([Izvješće, 26 Mar 2025, p. 194](https://split.hr/ukljuci-se/prostorno-planska-dokumentacija/izvjesca-o-javnim-raspravama/izvjesce-o-javnoj-raspravi-o-prijedlogu-izmjena-i-dopuna-generalnog-urbanistickog-plana-splita-i-strateskoj-studiji-o-utjecaju-na-okolis-izmjena-i-dopuna-generalnog-urbanistickog-plana-splita)).

No authentic interpretation (autentično tumačenje) of these articles exists, and no court ruling was found. An amendment procedure in 2015–2017 reached a konačni prijedlog (final proposal) that kept the same wording. It was never adopted, and its decision was repealed in 2022 ([Konačni prijedlog 2017](https://split.hr/gradska-uprava/gradsko-vijece/sjednice-gradskog-vijeca/lgs.axd?t=16&id=18388); [Izvješće, 26 Mar 2025, item 90](https://split.hr/DesktopModules/EasyDNNNews/DocumentDownload.ashx?portalid=0&moduleid=2192&articleid=20457&documentid=15067)).

**This corrects the project's earlier UPU report**, which said the obligation was "required since 2008". It was not. **The wait-for-the-plan mechanism was in the 2006 original, and in that version every rule 3.1 M1 plot needed a UPU.** The 2008 amendments replaced map 4.c, narrowed rule 3.1 to land with a mapped obligation, and added the in-dimensions and extension allowances for existing houses. The sheet in force today is the 2008 one; the 2014 amendment of map 4.c touched only Trstenik. Whether the 2006 sheet already covered residential Dračevac is unknown, because it was not found.

## Q3. Did the national law change? Yes, in 2014, and only for land a plan labels

**Answer: the statutory ban on new buildings before a UPU dates from 1 January 2014. It reaches only land a plan labels as neuređeno, urbana preobrazba or urbana sanacija. The 2026 law kept it and softened it slightly.** Whether a transitional rule caught rule 3.1 areas from 2014 to 2025 is an open question.

**Before 2007 there was no statutory ban.** The 1994 law left UPU obligations entirely to plans and required only that permits follow "dokument prostornog uređenja" (a planning document) ([ZPU NN 30/94](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/260702.html)). The one statutory freeze was coastal: from July 2004, "Unutar zaštićenoga obalnog pojasa ne može se graditi ako nije donesen urbanistički plan uređenja" (no building in the protected coastal belt without a UPU) ([NN 100/04](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2004_07_100_1897.html)).

**The 2007 law (ZPUG) introduced the idea.** It made a UPU obligatory for unbuilt parts and for parts "planiranih za urbanu obnovu" (planned for urban renewal). Where the law set that obligation, permits could be issued "samo na temelju tog plana" (only on the basis of that plan), with replacement and reconstruction exempt. It also let a council freeze permits pending a plan for **at most two years plus one** ([ZPUG NN 76/07, arts. 75, 125, 127](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2007_07_76_2395.html)).

**The present ban came in 2014** with the old ZPU, art. 79. Paragraph 1 makes a UPU obligatory "za neuređene dijelove građevinskog područja i za izgrađene dijelove tih područja planiranih za urbanu preobrazbu ili urbanu sanaciju" (for unserviced parts, and for built parts planned for transformation or sanation). Paragraph 2 removes the obligation where the PPUG or GUP sets conditions "s detaljnošću propisanom za urbanistički plan uređenja" (with UPU-level detail). **Paragraph 3 says that until then "ne može se izdati akt za građenje nove građevine"** (no permit may be issued for a new building). Paragraph 4 exempts reconstruction and like-for-like replacement ([ZPU NN 153/13](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2013_12_153_3220.html)).

The building-permit law mirrored this in ZoG art. 110(1) pt. 7: a permit requires that the UPU "je donesen … ako se dozvola izdaje na području za koje je posebnim zakonom propisana obveza njegova donošenja" (has been adopted, where a special law makes it obligatory) ([ZoG NN 153/13](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2013_12_153_3221.html)). Later amendments changed only wording ([NN 65/17](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2017_07_65_1494.html); [NN 39/19](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2019_04_39_801.html)), and NN 67/23 did not touch art. 79 ([NN 67/23](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2023_06_67_1101.html)).

The Ministry has applied the rule consistently. Its 2014 FAQ says permits "ne mogu se izdati … dok se taj plan ne donese" (cannot be issued until that plan is adopted) ([MPGI česta pitanja](https://mpgi.gov.hr/?id=176&page=1&url=print)), and its 2024 opinion to Tinjan repeats this ([MPGI, 21 Mar 2024](https://mpgi.gov.hr/UserDocsImages/dokumenti/Propisi/Upute_misljenja/2024_3_21.ZPU.79.2.pdf)). The county permit office applied it in Baška Voda, and the Split administrative court upheld the refusal ([UsIgr-268/2020-6](https://odluke.sudovi.hr/Document/View?id=a7677b81-269b-4dd7-bac5-07bb287edcff)).

**What the law does not do is decide which land falls into those categories.** The GUP "određuje" (determines) the neuređeni part and the part "planiran za urbanu preobrazbu i urbanu sanaciju" (old ZPU art. 78(1)). The permit checks refer to an obligation set "ovim Zakonom" (by this Law) or "posebnim zakonom" (by a special law). A UPU boundary that a GUP draws around ordinary built land is not such an obligation. It can block a permit only through the plan's own conditions (ZoG art. 110(1) pt. 3), not as a statutory bar ([ZPU NN 153/13](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2013_12_153_3220.html); [ZoG NN 153/13](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2013_12_153_3221.html)). On this reading, the statutory bar never reached residential east Split under the GUP in force, because the GUP never labelled it.

### The contested part: did a transitional rule catch rule 3.1 areas from 2014 to 2025?

Old ZPU art. 201 applied until plans were updated to the new categories, and it contained two deeming rules. Paragraph 2 provided that "neuređenim dijelom građevinskog područja smatraju se neizgrađeni dijelovi" (the unbuilt parts of plans in force count as neuređeno). Paragraph 3 provided that "dijelovi građevinskog područja određeni za urbanu obnovu prostornim planovima koji su na snazi" (parts that plans in force designate for urban renewal) count as planned for urbana preobrazba. There was no deeming rule for sanacija ([ZPU NN 153/13](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2013_12_153_3220.html)).

Rule 3.1's official title includes "urbana obnova", as a Split court judgment confirms ([UsIgr-256/2015-14](https://odluke.sudovi.hr/Document/View?id=e43be9b5-2c66-4c43-b70f-6bc107efb56a)). The City itself told objector no. 107 that the draft merely aligned "urbana obnova" with the law's "sanacija" and "preobrazba" ([Izvješće, 26 Mar 2025, pp. 213–214](https://split.hr/ukljuci-se/prostorno-planska-dokumentacija/izvjesca-o-javnim-raspravama/izvjesce-o-javnoj-raspravi-o-prijedlogu-izmjena-i-dopuna-generalnog-urbanistickog-plana-splita-i-strateskoj-studiji-o-utjecaju-na-okolis-izmjena-i-dopuna-generalnog-urbanistickog-plana-splita)). That gives a reasonable argument that 3.1 areas were deemed preobrazba, and therefore statutorily frozen, from 2014.

The counter-arguments are also real. A rule's title is not the same as land "određen za urbanu obnovu" (designated for urban renewal). Preobrazba means substantially changing the urban fabric, which fits a house district poorly. And no Ministry guidance or court decision addresses GUP rule titles.

**This report treats the question as open.** The practical fact is that **the City issued permits regardless** (Q5), and no one appears to have challenged them. Paragraph 2 raises a similar but narrower question for unbuilt plots, since a few permit points in the eastern suburbs sit on land the draft marks "neuređeno … prema PPUG-u Splita" (unserviced according to the PPUG, the City's town-wide spatial plan).

### From 1 January 2026 the bar continues, with one small new exit

NN 155/25 art. 106(2) requires a UPU for, among others, land "koji nisu izgrađeni i opremljeni osnovnom infrastrukturom" (not built and not served with basic infrastructure), and for "postojeće i izgrađene dijelove građevinskih područja za koje se planira urbana preobrazba i/ili urbana sanacija" (existing built parts for which transformation or sanation is planned). Until the UPU is adopted, a building permit can be issued only for reconstruction or like-for-like replacement (new ZoG art. 73(1) pt. 6 and 73(3)). The location-permit route adds one new exception: "građenje nove zgrade koja ima pristup na postojeću javnu prometnu površinu te mogućnost rješavanja odvodnje otpadnih voda prema mjesnim prilikama" (a new building with access to an existing public road and a local sewage solution) (new ZPU art. 180(2) pt. 3) ([ZPU NN 155/25](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2025_12_155_2315.html); [ZoG NN 155/25](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2025_12_155_2316.html)).

The new law has no deeming rule like art. 201. Its sanation and transformation category requires that the change be "planned", which is a plan designation, so on a literal reading the old GUP's unlabelled 4.c areas are not caught. The "not built and not serviced" category is worded as a fact rather than a designation, so it could catch empty unserviced plots even under an old plan. That is the law researcher's reading, and no guidance confirms it. Neither the old nor the new ZPU puts any time limit on the bar.

The register fits the literal reading. At least two new-build permits whose case numbers carry the year 2026, and which were therefore presumably filed and decided under the new law, were approved inside draft-UPU areas: UP/I-361-03/26-01/000088, a six-flat building in Kila approved on 29 June 2026 on land the draft shades sanacija, and UP/I-361-03/26-01/000097 in Orišac, approved on 14 September 2026 ([ISPU register](https://ispu.mgipu.hr/); [notes on the harvest](../research_notes/UPU%20building%20ban%20in%20sanation%20areas/split_permit_practice.md); the raw list of acts is not published because it gives the addresses of private permits).

| Period | Law | UPU obligatory by law for | Before the UPU |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1994 – Sep 2007 | ZPU NN 30/94 | Whatever a plan prescribes | No statutory ban, except the coastal belt from July 2004 |
| Oct 2007 – 2013 | ZPUG NN 76/07 | Unbuilt parts; parts planned for "urbana obnova" | Permits "samo na temelju tog plana"; replacement and reconstruction exempt; council freeze of at most 2 + 1 years |
| 2014 – 2025 | ZPU and ZoG NN 153/13 | Neuređeno; built parts planned for preobrazba or sanacija, unless the GUP gives UPU-level detail | **No permit for a new building** (79(3)); reconstruction and replacement exempt; art. 201 deeming rules |
| From 1 Jan 2026 | ZPU and ZoG NN 155/25 | Unbuilt and unserviced land; built parts planned for sanacija or preobrazba; and others | Building permit only for reconstruction or replacement; location permit also for a new building on an existing public road |

## Q4. What would the draft change? Explicit labels and fewer exits, by the City's choice

**Answer: the draft swaps the GUP's own test for the statutory categories and names the east-Split land as sanacija or neuređeno. That makes the wait explicit and statutory. It also removes the GUP's exits for existing houses, and elsewhere it loosens.** Once land is designated, the freeze on new buildings follows from statute. The designation itself, the choice of a UPU, and the list of exceptions are the City's choices.

The core is new čl. 103(1): *"Područja unutar obuhvata GUP-a na kojima je gradnja moguća samo temeljem prostornog plana užeg područja odnosno ne dozvoljava se neposredna provedba GUP-a, ako ovim odredbama ili Zakonom nije određeno drugačije, su: 1. neuređeni dijelovi … 2. izgrađeni dijelovi … planirani za urbanu preobrazbu 3. izgrađeni dijelovi … planirani za urbanu sanaciju"*. In English: in these three categories building is possible only under a narrower plan, with no direct application of the GUP, unless these provisions or the Law say otherwise. Other prescribed plans become recommendations: "do izrade prostornog plana užeg područja gradnja je moguća neposrednom provedbom GUP-a" (until the narrower plan is made, building is possible directly under the GUP) (čl. 103(4)). Old čl. 104 is deleted ([Odredbe, April 2025](https://split.hr/Portals/0/Dokumenti/Prostorno-planska/Izmjene%20i%20dopune%20Generalnog%20urbanisti%C4%8Dkog%20plana%20Splita%20za%20ponovnu%20javnu%20raspravu/1_%20Odredbe%20za%20provedbu.pdf)).

For existing houses the draft is stricter. The exception list in čl. 105(5) narrows to "manje" (smaller) infrastructure and drops the production and business exception. The in-dimensions allowance for reconstruction until the plan is gone, and so is the low-consolidated sentence allowing extensions and raising. So are the relaxed conditions for built parts: plots up to 40% smaller, Po+P+3, 1 m from the boundary, and paying instead of providing parking. Under new čl. 49.b(2), "rekonstrukcija postojećih građevina dozvoljena je pod istim uvjetima propisanim ovim Planom za izgradnju novih građevina" (reconstruction is allowed under the same conditions as new building). These changes apply citywide but bite hardest in the east.

The City says this is deliberate. Legalisation from 2012 gave Split "cca 15000 'novih postojećih' građevina ali koje su protivne planskim odredbama" (about 15,000 "new existing" buildings that contradict the plan). The rules were tightened "kako se, sada zakonitim ali 'neplanskim' građevinama, ne bi omogućile nove nadogradnje i dogradnje" (so that these now-legal but "unplanned" buildings cannot add new floors or extensions) ([Obrazloženje, April 2025, p. 25](https://split.hr/Portals/0/Dokumenti/Prostorno-planska/Izmjene%20i%20dopune%20Generalnog%20urbanisti%C4%8Dkog%20plana%20Splita%20za%20ponovnu%20javnu%20raspravu/3_%20Obrazlozenje.pdf)).

### Is the freeze required by law? The consequence is; the designation is not

The City presents the change as the law's doing: "na područjima na kojima je Zakonom prozvana obveza donošenja UPU-a, prije njegovog donošenja ne može se izdati lokacijska dozvola i građevinska dozvola za građenje nove građevine, dok je na područjima planske obveze preporuke … izdavanje navedenih akata moguće i prije donošenja tog plana". In English: where the Law requires a UPU, no permits for new buildings before it; where the plan only recommends one, permits are possible ([Obrazloženje, §2.1.1.4, p. 6](https://split.hr/Portals/0/Dokumenti/Prostorno-planska/Izmjene%20i%20dopune%20Generalnog%20urbanisti%C4%8Dkog%20plana%20Splita%20za%20ponovnu%20javnu%20raspravu/3_%20Obrazlozenje.pdf)). To the county, it said the new UPUs have "status sanacijskog plana ili plana urbane preobrazbe što je zakonom propisana obveza" (the status of sanation or transformation plans, which is a legal obligation) ([Izvješće, 26 Mar 2025, p. 323](https://split.hr/ukljuci-se/prostorno-planska-dokumentacija/izvjesca-o-javnim-raspravama/izvjesce-o-javnoj-raspravi-o-prijedlogu-izmjena-i-dopuna-generalnog-urbanistickog-plana-splita-i-strateskoj-studiji-o-utjecaju-na-okolis-izmjena-i-dopuna-generalnog-urbanistickog-plana-splita)).

That is **correct about the consequence and incomplete about the cause**. The law requires "mjere za urbanu sanaciju" (sanation measures) where legalised buildings predominate (old ZPU art. 53(5); new art. 77(5)), but it does not require those measures to take the form of a UPU. It also lets the GUP set UPU-level conditions itself, which removes both the UPU obligation and the freeze (old art. 79(2); new art. 106(4)) ([ZPU NN 153/13](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2013_12_153_3220.html); [ZPU NN 155/25](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2025_12_155_2315.html)). That route has a price: the Ministry reads it as requiring text and maps "u mjerilu propisanom za urbanistički plan uređenja" (at the scale prescribed for a UPU) ([MPGI, 21 Mar 2024](https://mpgi.gov.hr/UserDocsImages/dokumenti/Propisi/Upute_misljenja/2024_3_21.ZPU.79.2.pdf)).

The exception list in čl. 105(5) is the City's own. The law researcher reads it as arguably *more* generous than statute for streets and public buildings, though that has not been tested. It says nothing about houses, which keep only the statutory reconstruction and replacement exits through "ili Zakonom".

The City's preference is on record. The county had warned that such plans "često … ostaju 'mrtvo slovo na papiru'" (often stay a dead letter). The City replied that a GUP acting as an implementing plan is "anomalija u sustavu planiranja" (an anomaly in the planning system) and that narrower plans "treba biti što više" (there should be as many as possible) ([Izvješće, 26 Mar 2025, p. 148](https://split.hr/ukljuci-se/prostorno-planska-dokumentacija/izvjesca-o-javnim-raspravama/izvjesce-o-javnoj-raspravi-o-prijedlogu-izmjena-i-dopuna-generalnog-urbanistickog-plana-splita-i-strateskoj-studiji-o-utjecaju-na-okolis-izmjena-i-dopuna-generalnog-urbanistickog-plana-splita)).

### How much buildable land the labels would hold back

The bar does not touch what is already built. The draft's labels cover **681 ha** citywide (95 ha urbana sanacija, 309 ha urbana preobrazba and 277 ha neuređeno), counting only fill the sheet shows, inside the GUP boundary and outside plans in force. Much of that is built up: streets, legalised houses, the shipyard. What the bar would hold back is the land still free for a new building. The project site counts it parcel by parcel, with the same model as its `/gup` chart. It must also be large enough, together with free neighbouring land, for a new building plot. Public-use zones are left out because public buildings may still be built before the UPU.

## Q6. So why would there be a ban now? The same land would change legal category

**Not because the law changed in 2026.** NN 155/25 continues the 2014 rule and even adds a small exit for plots on existing roads. The ban would come from **moving the same land from one kind of UPU obligation to another**.

Since 2006, residential east Split has lived under a **plan-made** obligation: the 4.c boundary, enforced only by the GUP's own wording. The City's permit office has read that wording as porous, whether through rule 3.1's "a za ostalo temeljem ovog Plana", the unqualified extension sentence, or both. The draft would put the same land into a **law-made** category by labelling it "planirano za urbanu sanaciju" or "neuređeno".

Once that happens, the national permit law itself asks whether the UPU has been adopted (new ZoG art. 73(1) pt. 6; new ZPU art. 180(1) pt. 4), and an officer cannot answer that by reinterpreting a sentence of the GUP. The only exits are reconstruction and like-for-like replacement, plus a location-permit exit for a new building on an existing road whose reach for family houses is unclear. The same draft removes the GUP's own allowances for extending legal houses, and ties reconstruction to new-build conditions that many legalised houses cannot meet. **Same map, different legal machinery.** The City's own §2.1.1.4 draws exactly this line between a statutory obligation (no permits) and a plan recommendation (permits possible).

**The disagreement between text and practice has a practical answer: the City has not applied a freeze.** On paper, "forbidden" is the more defensible reading of the GUP in force for a new house on a 3.1 plot inside a 4.c boundary, and the City's April 2025 Obrazloženje implies the same. Yet the City's register shows about 57 approvals, six in Dračevac, and extensions beyond existing dimensions, with no refusal on that ground. The City's reply to objection no. 73 also treats rule 3.1's parameters as governing building until the plans arrive. What residents live under is the practice. That practice, however, rests on a contested reading rather than a clear rule. It holds as long as the office keeps reading the text that way, and it would end automatically once the draft's labels are adopted.

**Whether a statutory ban already applied from 2014 to 2025 is unresolved.** The strongest reading of the old ZPU confines its bar to land a plan labels, and the GUP in force labels nothing in east Split. But art. 201(3) deemed "urbana obnova" areas to be preobrazba, and rule 3.1 is officially titled "…urbana obnova…". If that deeming reached 3.1 areas, many permits issued there between 2014 and 2025 would have been issued contrary to statute. No court or ministry has said so, and the City issued them regardless.

**Two statements in the project's earlier UPU report need qualifying.** Its claims that "the freeze is already the City's rule" and "exists today on paper" should read: *the freeze is arguably in the text of the GUP in force, but the City has not applied it; an enforced freeze would arrive with the draft's sanacija and neuređeno labels.* Its claim that the obligation was "required since 2008" should read: *the mechanism dates from 2006; in 2008 the City replaced the map and loosened the rule.*

**Why would the City choose this?** Its stated reasons are the 15,000 legalised "novih postojećih" buildings and the goals it sets the sanation UPUs in draft čl. 106(2): streets, utilities, public space, and rules for legalised houses, including "uklanjanje (uz mogućnost nove gradnje) neuvjetnih građevina" (removing substandard buildings, with the option of new building) ([Odredbe, April 2025](https://split.hr/Portals/0/Dokumenti/Prostorno-planska/Izmjene%20i%20dopune%20Generalnog%20urbanisti%C4%8Dkog%20plana%20Splita%20za%20ponovnu%20javnu%20raspravu/1_%20Odredbe%20za%20provedbu.pdf)). Those goals are legitimate.

The risk is duration. Since 2014 the statutory bar has had no time limit, unlike the two-plus-one-year cap on council freezes under the 2007 law ([ZPUG NN 76/07](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2007_07_76_2395.html)), and the draft gives the prescribed UPUs neither funding nor a timetable. The High Administrative Court has annulled plan provisions that made building depend on a future UPU "čije je donošenje neizvjesno" (whose adoption is uncertain), citing legal certainty ([VUS Usoz-73/16, NN 46/2017](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/full/2017_05_46_1091.html)). The European benchmark holds that long building prohibitions without review or compensation can breach the right to property ([Sporrong and Lönnroth v. Sweden](https://echrlawyer.org/echr-case-law/sporrong-and-lonnroth-v-sweden/)). A freeze that follows directly from statute cannot be struck down in a plan review as "contrary to law", but the designation choice behind it can still be argued as disproportionate.

## Conclusion: what residents can do before 16 November 2026

**The lever is the label, not the law.** In the City's call for initiatives (1 October – 16 November 2026), residents of Dračevac 2 and the built parts of Mostine can ask for three things, in order of preference. First, **that residential Dračevac 2 be shown as "preporuka"** on map 4.d: a blue outline with no sanacija fill, as the draft already does for about 21 ha of Mostine and Harakovac and for part of Dračevac 2 itself. Direct building under the GUP's parameters would then continue while the UPU is prepared. Failing that, **that the GUP set UPU-level conditions** (street lines and plot rules) for these streets, so that no statutory UPU obligation arises (new ZPU art. 106(4)). If the City insists on sanacija, **that the GUP state explicit prijelazne mjere** (transitional measures, new ZPU art. 105(2) pt. 3). These should cover reconstruction, like-for-like replacement and extensions of legalised houses within rule 3.1's parameters, restore the in-dimensions and low-consolidated extension sentences, and say how the new location-permit exit for plots on existing roads applies.

Whichever option is taken, the request should also ask for a deadline and a budget line for UPU Dračevac 2, and should ask which procedure the call feeds: the 2021 amendment procedure behind the April 2025 draft, or a new-generation plan.

**Second, find out what the permit office actually relies on.** A request under the Zakon o pravu na pristup informacijama (the freedom-of-information law) to the Upravni odjel za urbanizam would settle it. It should ask for the reasoning of UP/I-361-03/20-01/000010 (17 March 2021, a new building on draft sanacija land), UP/I-361-03/19-01/000158 (4 May 2023, three flats on draft sanacija land) and UP/I-361-03/22-01/000029 (29 December 2022, a second floor added), and for the grounds of the Dračevac location-permit refusal of 10 April 2025 (UP/I-350-05/23-01/000050). If the office cites rule 3.1's "za ostalo" or the low-consolidated extension sentence, residents would hold documentary proof that the GUP in force works as a building regime, not a freeze, and a concrete reason to ask that the draft keep those provisions.

**Third, owners with a project on draft sanacija land should know** that, on current practice, a route exists today that the draft would close. Legal advice matters there, because it is not established what happens to an application still pending when the amendments take effect.
